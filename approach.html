Teaching approach

Show the motion. Then ask for proof.

Most geometry is taught as a list of facts to memorise. Mathematics in Motion animates the figure so the student sees the result happen, then checks that they can use it before moving on.

Three design rules

1

One idea per video

Under two minutes. If a theorem needs a second idea, that is a second lesson. Short videos get rewatched; long ones get skipped.

2

The figure moves

A point slides along an arc and the angle does not change. A chord rotates until it becomes a tangent. The motion is the explanation.

3

Answers, not choices

Students type a number or an expression. No multiple choice, so a guess cannot pass for understanding. Hints and worked solutions open when needed.

The mastery path

The roadmap for every lesson once accounts exist. It borrows the "restrict access" gating of Moodle, the "M of N" mastery rule of Kolibri, and Khan Academy's habit of making the rule visible to the student.

Watch

The animation, with the key question posed on screen.

Worked examples

Two or three examples with every step shown. At least three per skill, so a struggling student can always see one more.

Practise

Questions drawn from a pool, so a retry gets new numbers and a new diagram. Instant feedback, one hint per miss.

Mastery check

Five fresh questions. 4 of 5 opens the next lesson. The rule is printed on the page, not hidden.

What is live today. Watch and Practise, scored in the browser for the current visit. Worked examples are inside each question. The mastery check and gating need saved progress, which needs accounts.

Roadmap

Now

Static showcase

This site. Video, practice questions, textbook alignment. Free to host on GitHub Pages. Jephias edits content as plain HTML.

Next

Gated path with accounts

Student sign-in, saved progress, the 4-of-5 mastery check and a teacher gradebook. A free-tier backend such as Supabase keeps the cost at zero. Parent consent is required for students under 13.

Later

Checkpoints and skill levels

Questions pinned to moments in the video, Khan-style skill levels across a chapter, and an AI tutor that gives hints but never the answer.
